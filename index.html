AI tooling

I started on my own time in 2022, when Midjourney was still on v2. None of it was in my job description — I went looking because the manual steps were what slowed the line down.

  • Marketing pack collation. Every pack draws the same kinds of material from the same places. I used AI tools to sort and prepare that material, so building a pack stopped being a manual pass through every file.
  • Promo video storyboards. Early-stage boards for the GPVs — what a video needs to show, in what order, and roughly how it should look — worked out before a single frame of footage got captured.
  • Handing it to the team. I was the first person at the studio testing any of this, so I wrote up what held up in production and what didn't, and brought it to my colleagues as a report rather than a rumour. The tools spread because someone had already done the testing.

Technical art

Where the art job stops being a drawing job.

Portrait to landscape conversion. The title existed in portrait only. I was the only artist on the conversion, working with one developer to rebuild the UI, reel layout and symbol arrangement for a different aspect ratio.

Every decision had to be checked against what the engine could actually do, so most of the real work was deciding where the design had to bend and where it couldn't.

Alongside that: naming and structuring assets so the same layout could be handed to someone else, and keeping the documentation current so it stayed usable after I moved on.

One background, two orientations. Phones play this title both ways up, and each orientation shows a different slice of the same background. So the art is drawn well past the edges of the screen in every direction: the centre is what everybody sees, and the extensions cover whatever the device crops to.

The composition has to survive both crops. The focal point needs to read in a tall narrow frame and in a short wide one, and the background has to keep making sense wherever it gets cut — without drawing it twice.

The same dialog, laid out twice. A win summary carries fixed content — what you defeated, what you won, how many free spins — and two very different shapes to fit it into. Portrait stacks it. Landscape spreads it.

Same elements, same hierarchy, different arrangement. Both versions get worked out at mock-up stage, so the landscape layout isn't an afterthought squeezed in once the portrait one is signed off.
